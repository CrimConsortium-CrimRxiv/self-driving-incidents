About the data

What this dataset is, how each case is found and coded, what it cannot tell you, and how to use and cite it.

We're iteratively improving this site and dataset. Please email corrections or suggestions to consortium@crimrxiv.com.

What it is

A case is one incident in which a vehicle's automated-driving feature is claimed, questioned or confirmed to be involved: a crash, a near miss, a police stop, a person found asleep at the wheel, or a court case about any of these. Incidents with no collision are included. The dataset covers Tesla (Autopilot, Full Self-Driving, Summon and its robotaxi service) for now, and is built so other makers can be added.

It is meant for research and teaching in criminology and related fields: who is said to have done what, how police, prosecutors, regulators and courts respond, and how responsibility is attributed when a machine shares the driving.

How cases are found

Once a day, a program searches Google News and Bing News for reports that name an automated-driving feature and describe something that happened. Reports about the same event are grouped into one case, and later reports (an arrest, a lawsuit, a verdict) are attached to the case they belong to. Round-ups of several incidents, statistics, product news and opinion pieces are set aside and listed, with the reason, in screened_out.csv.

The searches, the rules for keeping a report, the time period covered and the known gaps are set out in full on the sampling page.

How cases are coded

A language model reads the reports attached to a case (the article text where the publisher allows it, otherwise the headline and summary) and drafts the coded variables, a short neutral narrative, and a list of who said what. Three rules limit what it can assert:

  • It may use only the reports attached to the case.
  • Every coded value must come with a quotation, and the program keeps the value only if that quotation is found word for word in the report. Values that fail are removed and counted on the case page.
  • A mention of a feature is never treated as evidence that it was in use. The variable engagement_status separates what an official source or the maker's own data states from what a driver, lawyer or witness claims.

Every record is marked “machine-coded, not yet reviewed” until a person has checked it. Summaries shown on the homepage are each publisher's own words, not ours.

What it cannot tell you

  • It is not a count of all incidents. It holds only incidents that reached published news in English and that our searches found. Many incidents are never reported.
  • Counts are not rates. Nothing here says how often these features are used without incident, so the data cannot show whether they are safer or less safe than a human driver.
  • It does not establish fault or cause. It records what coverage reports and who says so.
  • Machine coding makes mistakes. Dates, places and counts can be wrong, and two cases may turn out to be the same event. Check the linked sources before relying on a record.
  • Coverage changes. Early reports are often revised. A case reflects the reports attached to it on the date shown.

Privacy

Coded fields, narratives and claims describe people by role (“the driver”, “a passenger”) and do not name private individuals. Names may still appear in publishers' headlines and in the linked reports. This site and the download hold links and short quotations, not copies of articles. So that the coding can be checked, we keep the text of the reports we were able to read in a private archive; it is not part of the dataset and is not redistributed. To ask for a change, email consortium@crimrxiv.com.

Download, licence and citation

Download everything as one .zip, or take single files: cases.csv, evidence.csv, claims.csv, sources.csv, screened_out.csv, cases.json, codebook.json.

The data are free to reuse under CC BY 4.0. The code that builds them is on GitHub, where every change to the data is recorded.

Compiled from published news reports found through Google News and Bing News searches, refreshed daily (how cases are sampled). Coded values are drafted by a language model from those reports and are not yet reviewed by a person; each one links to the passage it rests on. A count of incidents is not a rate, and a mention of an automated-driving feature is not a finding that it was in use. Verify every case at its sources.
